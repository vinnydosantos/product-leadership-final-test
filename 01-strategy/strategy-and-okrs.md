# Craft an Advanced Product Strategy, Module 1 Lab

## Write your one-page strategy
- **Winning aspiration: define winning in the customer's terms, not internal metrics. What does success look like for the specific person you serve?:** For a foreman on a Meridian-managed construction site, winning means getting a job-site blocker to the person who can resolve it, knowing who owns the next action, and keeping the crew moving without chasing group texts or re-entering the same update for the office.
- **Where to play: segment, geography, and use case, explicitly. Which customers, markets, and surfaces will you focus on?:** Start with foremen and field superintendents on $50M+ US construction projects run by Meridian’s existing general-contractor customers. Focus on the mobile, on-site workflow for reporting a work-blocking issue with a photo and location, getting an accountable owner, and tracking resolution. Connect that workflow to the project manager’s existing Meridian workspace.
Initial boundary: serve existing enterprise job sites first; defer acquiring $5M–$50M mid-market customers until the field workflow proves useful and earns repeat use.
- **How to win: your differentiator. What can you do that your specific competitors cannot easily replicate?:** Turn a field photo into an owned, trackable path to resolution using the project context already in Meridian. A foreman captures the blocker once; Meridian connects it to the relevant subcontractor, RFI, or document, routes it to a responsible person, and returns the next action to the field.
Phone cameras and group texts make reporting easy; our bet is to make getting an answer and acting on it easier. Mobile-first competitors can copy the capture experience, but reproducing Meridian’s established project records, enterprise workflows, and customer relationships would take longer.
This advantage depends on project owners responding reliably; routing alone will not unblock a crew.
- **Capabilities required: what you must be world-class at. What will you build, buy, or partner for?:** Fast, reliable field capture: Build a photo-and-location reporting flow with minimal typing, offline capture, and reliable sync. Validate it with foremen using their phones on active sites
Project context and accountable routing: Build on Meridian’s existing records and permissions to connect blockers to subcontractors, RFIs, and documents. Start with explicit routing rules and manual reassignment
Resolution that reaches the crew: Build acknowledgment, next-action ownership, escalation, and field confirmation that work can resume. A closed office ticket must not automatically mean a resolved field problem
Dependable mobile infrastructure: Buy/reuse proven storage, upload, and notification services, subject to enterprise security requirements, so the ten-person team can focus on the workflow
Responsive job-site operations: Partner with existing contractor customers: foremen test the experience; superintendents and project managers establish response responsibilities and escalation coverage. Software cannot compensate for an owner who never responds.
- **Management systems: the metrics and rituals that keep your choices alive quarter to quarter.:** Daily — Site owners: Review unacknowledged blockers, missing owners, and overdue next actions against agreed response windows. Reassign or escalate issues so reports lead to action.
Weekly — Product team and pilot sites: Review median and 90th-percentile time from reporting to field-confirmed work resumption, plus reopened issues. Examine the longest delays with foremen and project managers to identify where the workflow breaks.
Monthly — Product lead and Customer Success: Track repeat use by foreman cohort, the share of sampled blockers handled end to end in Meridian, and duplicate reporting through texts or office re-entry. Determine whether Meridian is replacing the informal workflow.
Quarterly — Leadership: Compare pilot results with pre-pilot baselines, assess support burden and enterprise workflow reliability, and challenge the strategic assumptions. Expand only when repeat use and faster resolution hold across sites; otherwise adjust or stop the bet.

Establish baselines and decision thresholds before the pilot. App opens and uploaded photos alone will not count as success.

## Name your one hard no
- **Your one hard no:** We will not pursue new $5M–$50M mid-market customers during the initial rollout, despite the churn pressure in that segment. We will concentrate our ten-person team on proving that foremen at existing enterprise sites can get blockers resolved faster and choose to use Meridian again. Acquisition-driven requirements would spread that team across a second market before we have earned adoption in the field.

## Write your three OKRs
- **Objective:** Make Meridian the workflow foremen trust to get job-site blockers resolved and keep crews moving on existing US enterprise projects
- **KR1:** Faster resolution: Reduce median time from blocker reporting to field-confirmed work resumption from 8 to 4 working hours by the end of the 90-day pilot
- **KR2:** Earned repeat use: Increase four-week retained usage among activated pilot foremen from 20% to 60%, defined as using Meridian to report or follow up on a blocker in at least three of the four weeks after activation
- **KR3:** Less duplicate work: Reduce the share of Meridian-reported blockers requiring duplicate reporting through group texts or office re-entry from 70% to 25% by pilot end, verified through weekly sampling with field and office users

## AI pressure-test
- **Which challenge from the AI is most valid, and why?:** The most valid challenge is that we chose blocker resolution as our entry point without evidence that communication and ownership failures are a major cause of field delays. The scenario confirms low adoption, but it does not explain which unmet need would make foremen switch from photos and group texts.
If crews are mainly waiting for materials or engineering decisions, better routing may document the wait without shortening it. That would undermine both our promise to keep crews moving and our resolution-time KR. Before committing, I would review recent blocker incidents with foremen and project managers to identify where clearer ownership and faster communication could actually have changed the outcome.
- **What would you change based on the pushback, and what would you defend?:** I would change the initial commitment from building the full blocker workflow to validating it against recent incidents at pilot sites. I would identify delays caused by missing ownership or slow communication, then test whether Meridian can shorten them. I would replace assumed baselines with observed data and measure repeat use among foremen who actually encounter recurring blockers.

I would also sharpen the hard no: defer new advanced analytics and deeper ERP enhancements during the pilot, while maintaining existing enterprise features and contractual commitments. This protects the team from the competing investment direction.

I would defend the narrow field focus, the additive approach, and field-confirmed resolution. Foremen need a dependable answer and next action, and success should mean work can resume—not merely that someone in the office closed a ticket.
