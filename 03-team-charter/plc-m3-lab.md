## Refine and complete your charter
- **What We Own. What this team owns.:** The ten-person Foundations team owns field adoption on existing US enterprise projects while preserving enterprise functionality. PM 1 owns the three pilot bets: annotated photos, four-field daily logs, and crew-relevant schedule notifications, measured through retained field usage and duplicate reporting. PM 2 owns enterprise requirements, existing contractual obligations, and continuity. The Engineering Lead owns technical delivery and reliability. The Foundations Product Leader owns priorities and capacity allocation. Customer site owners remain responsible for responding to blockers and confirming work can resume.
- **What is out of scope.:** This quarter: custom executive dashboards, a new time-tracking module, and automated compliance checklist generation. New mid-market acquisition and a platform rebuild are also excluded. Existing contractual obligations and necessary repairs remain in scope. Next/Later roadmap placement does not authorize development; starting those items requires an explicit prioritization decision.
- **Cross-boundary decisions that need a joint call.:** PM 1, PM 2, and the Engineering Lead must jointly review changes to enterprise-required daily-log information, shared data or permissions, integrations, and engineering allocation. The Foundations Product Leader decides any resulting priority conflict. If pilot connectivity makes the selected workflows unusable, this group assesses a minimum offline capability to replace schedule notifications in Now, keeping three commitments. Renewal requests cannot bypass this review.
- **How We Decide. Who decides feature and scope calls.:** PM 1 decides field scope within approved bets and capacity; PM 2 decides enterprise scope within approved obligations and capacity. Engineering decides implementation and validates effort and feasibility. The Foundations Product Leader decides trade-offs between these areas. Removing a Now bet, adding an excluded initiative, or changing an approved customer commitment requires explicit approval at the appropriate authority level. Sales may communicate a delivery promise only after Product and Engineering approve it.
- **How cross-team conflicts escalate.:** The requesting PM records the need, evidence, deadline, contractual status, estimated effort, and work that would be displaced. Within one business day, both PMs, Engineering, and the Foundations Product Leader hold a synchronous call; the leader records a decision that day or escalates it to the executive sponsor. The sponsor or a designated delegate decides within two business days of escalation. Until then, approved allocations remain unchanged. Active outages, data loss, or security incidents permit immediate Engineering-led intervention, with prompt notification and a priority review by the next business day.
- **Who resolves escalations from outside the team.:** The Foundations Product Leader receives requests from Sales, Customer Success, and other leaders and resolves them within the approved strategy and budget. The executive sponsor resolves requests requiring additional funding, reversal of a strategic exclusion, or acceptance of renewal risk. For the dashboard tied to two at-risk renewals, approval must name the field work displaced, its expected impact, and any customer commitments affected before engineers are reassigned.

## Show and swap your team charter
- **Where does the charter leave room for interpretation that could cause a conflict?:** The biggest ambiguity is “approved capacity”: the charter does not specify how much engineering capacity is protected for field adoption, so PM 2 could interpret a renewal request as fitting within enterprise capacity while PM 1 sees it displacing field work. It also leaves “existing contractual obligations” open to interpretation; a sales promise is not necessarily a signed obligation.
